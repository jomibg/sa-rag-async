Deep within the Whispering Woods lived an old tailor named Alistair. Unlike ordinary tailors who worked with silk or wool, Alistair spun thread from the words people left behind—the soft secrets, the sharp rumors, and the heavy sighs carried by the forest wind.

One chilly autumn evening, a young prince arrived at his cottage door. The prince was burdened by a heavy heart; he was to inherit a kingdom he did not yet understand. "Weave me a coat," the prince demanded, "that will make me look wise and formidable to my subjects."

Alistair nodded, catching the prince's anxious sighs as they floated toward the hearth fire. He went to his loom and began to weave. He used the golden threads of forgotten promises for the lining, the dark wool of late-night worries for the collar, and the silver sheen of quiet hopes for the sleeves.

For three days and nights, the loom hummed. When Alistair finished, he presented a cloak of shimmering, shifting colors. It was beautiful, but when the prince draped it over his shoulders, he gasped. It felt as light as a feather, yet he could hear a gentle, low murmur—the collective hopes and fears of his entire kingdom, echoing softly in the fabric.

"This is no armor of stone," the prince whispered, looking at his reflection.

"No," Alistair replied softly. "True wisdom does not block out the voices of your people; it wears them close to the heart. Listen to them, and you will never rule alone."
