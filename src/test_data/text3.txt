Mable possessed a lantern that burned not with oil, but with curiosity. It was a brass contraption passed down from her grandmother, and its light had a peculiar magic: it only illuminated things that were truly lost.

If you dropped a copper coin in the tall grass, Mable’s lantern would cast a bright blue glow upon it. If a traveler lost his way in the misty moors, the lantern’s beam would pierce the fog, revealing the forgotten path home.

One day, a sorrowful giant came knocking at her door. He was so tall his head brushed the clouds, but his shoulders were slumped with grief. "Little spark," the giant rumbled, "I have lost my joy. Can your lantern find it?"

Mable lifted the brass handle and blew a breath of curiosity into the glass. A soft, warm amber light bloomed. She walked ahead of the giant, casting the light across the valleys and over the steep mountain peaks.

They searched for hours. The lantern did not shine on the giant’s old treasures, nor did it light up his grand stone castle. Instead, as they crossed a blooming meadow, the amber beam suddenly focused on a tiny, injured sparrow caught in a bramble bush.

Mable looked up at the giant. He knelt down, his massive hands incredibly gentle as he untangled the bird and held it close. The sparrow chirped, shivering, before nestling into his palm. The giant’s face softened, and a rumbling, hearty laugh shook the trees.

The amber light of the lantern quietly went out.

"I see," the giant whispered, a tear of happiness falling to the earth. "My joy wasn't lost in the clouds. It was just waiting for me to look down and care for something small."
